Standard Work Exercise

This activity demonstrates why simply giving people instructions does not always produce a consistent result.

Same taskEveryone follows the same sequence of instructions.
Different interpretationsSome wording may be understood differently by different people.
Learning goalExplore how standard work can reduce variation and improve consistency.
Important: There is no need to make your drawing “perfect”. Follow the instructions as you understand them.
Before you begin

How the activity works

  • You will work on a digital drawing grid using a mouse, trackpad, stylus or touchscreen.
  • You will receive 13 instructions, one at a time.
  • Complete each instruction on the same drawing before moving to the next.
  • You may use Undo if you make an accidental mark, but try not to redesign earlier steps simply because a later instruction changes your interpretation.
  • At the end, you will save an image of your drawing and share it in the online class chat for comparison.
The exercise is about variation in interpretation. Different drawings are expected—and that is part of the learning.
Drawing activity

Follow the standard work instructions

Tip: On a touchscreen or tablet, draw directly with your finger or stylus. On a computer, hold the mouse button while drawing.

What are we looking for?

  • Do all drawings look the same?
  • Which instructions created the most variation?
  • Would a diagram, exact dimension, template or clearer wording reduce that variation?

Conclusion

What does this demonstrate?

Same instructions do not necessarily create the same result.

When instructions are vague, open to interpretation or difficult to visualise, different people can produce different outputs even when everyone is trying to follow the process correctly.

This is why Standard Work matters in Lean: the method should be clear, repeatable and sufficiently detailed so that trained people can perform the same task consistently.

Lean connectionStandard work reduces unnecessary variation in how work is performed.
Six Sigma connectionDifferent outputs from the same process are a simple example of process variation.
People vs processInconsistent results may reflect an unclear system—not poor individual performance.
Class discussion: If we wanted everyone to produce a much more similar drawing, what would we change about the instructions or the work standard?
